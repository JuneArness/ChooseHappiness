# Moderation and escalation playbook (DRAFT: needs professional and legal review)

This is a starting draft. It must be reviewed by a qualified mental health professional and a lawyer, and adapted to the people and hours you actually have, before launch.

## What moderators are, and aren't
- Moderators keep the community safe and kind. They are **not** counselors, crisis workers or clinicians.
- Moderators never diagnose, give medical advice, or promise to stay with someone in crisis.
- The community is **not** monitored around the clock unless you staff it that way. The site must say what's true (Admin → Settings).

## The queue (Admin → Moderation)
Items appear in this order:
1. **Urgent:** posts held because they may mention self-harm or suicide, and reports marked "Someone may be at risk".
2. New members' first posts.
3. Other reports.
4. Appeals.

## Possible risk of self-harm or suicide
1. Read the post fully. Don't approve it just to clear the queue.
2. **If there's an immediate threat** (a plan, a time, means at hand) and you know the person's location, follow your emergency procedure and call 911. *(Professional/legal review: what information you have, when you may share it, and who decides.)*
3. Send a short, caring message from the website support chat or a reply, for example:
   > "Thank you for sharing this. I'm a volunteer moderator, not a counselor, but I want you to have support right now. You can call or text 988 any time (U.S.), or chat at chat.988lifeline.org. If you're in immediate danger, please call 911."
4. Decide whether the post is safe to publish:
   - **Approve** when it shares struggle without methods or graphic detail and can receive supportive replies. Consider asking the author to add a content warning.
   - **Keep it hidden (remove)** when it includes methods, plans, graphic detail, or could put readers at risk. Tell the author kindly why, and repeat the crisis resources.
5. Record what you did in the resolution note. Tell a second team member about urgent cases.
6. Look after yourself. Hand off difficult cases.

## Other reports
| Situation | Usual action |
|---|---|
| Harassment, hate, threats | Remove; suspend for repeats or anything serious; ban for threats or hate |
| Sharing someone's private details | Remove at once |
| Spam or selling | Remove; ban obvious spam accounts |
| Harmful health misinformation (e.g. "stop your medication") | Remove; explain kindly |
| Disagreement or bluntness that isn't abusive | Dismiss; consider a gentle reminder of the guidelines |

Escalation ladder: content removal → warning → suspension (days) → ban (administrators only).

## Private messages
Moderators can only see a private message when it is reported, and each view is written to the audit log. Only open what you need to decide the report.

## Appeals
A different team member than the one who acted should review each appeal when possible. Reverse the decision if it was a mistake, and restore the content or reactivate the account.

## Records
Everything you do is written to the audit log automatically. Don't copy members' private content outside the platform.
